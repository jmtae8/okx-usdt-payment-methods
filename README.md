## After the purchase: turning USDT into another coin

Inside the exchange, the low-friction option is **Trade → Convert**, which swaps at a quoted rate and settles immediately. Use **Spot** instead if you want to place a limit order and set your own entry price.

If your USDT sits in the OKX Wallet rather than your trading account, the in-app path is **Wallet → Trade → Swap**, where you pick the chain holding your USDT, set From to USDT, choose the token you want, and enter an amount. The first swap from a given wallet requires an **Approve USDT** transaction before the swap itself; after that you can swap directly. Slippage and routing can be adjusted or left at default.

The DeFi route has three costs that exchange swaps don't. Network fees on some chains are expensive. Slippage is real when the price moves between quote and execution. And a DEX swap can only give you tokens that exist on the same chain, which is the limitation people trip over most often. USDT sitting in an Ethereum wallet can be swapped for ETH or any other ERC-20 token, but not for Bitcoin, because Bitcoin isn't on Ethereum. If the token you want lives on another chain, you're looking at a bridge, not a swap.

OKX's DEX aggregator routes orders across more than 70 DeFi exchanges to improve pricing and liquidity, and the web flow is **OKX Web3 DEX swap → connect wallet → Trade → Swap → set the From network and USDT → set the To network and token → enter the amount → approve → swap**. Confirmation typically takes a few minutes.

USDT itself is issued on several chains, including Ethereum, Tron, Solana, Algorand and BSC, so the same ticker can exist in five places in your account and only one of them is the right one for a given destination.

## Does OKX charge a fee when you withdraw USDT?

No platform fee, but you still pay the network. OKX's wording is specific: some exchanges, OKX included, don't charge a fee to transfer USDT, but a network fee applies depending on the network you choose. That fee goes to the blockchain, not to OKX, and it's charged whether you're sending from a wallet to an exchange or between two wallets you control.

The withdrawal flow is **Assets → Withdraw → select USDT → on-chain withdrawal → enter address, network and amount → Continue → authenticate with your account security settings**. Funds arrive in minutes, varying with the network's congestion.

Two fields on that screen cause almost all permanent losses: the address and the network. A USDT deposit sent on Tron to an Ethereum address is gone, and no support ticket recovers it. ## Two risks that belong in the price

USDT is a stablecoin, not a guarantee. OKX acknowledges in its own FAQ that volatility has previously caused USDT to lose its dollar peg. Treat a depeg as a low-probability event with a real precedent rather than something that can't happen.

The second risk only applies if you're not buying with USD. Purchase USDT with another currency and the amount you receive moves with that currency's exchange rate against the dollar, on top of any fee. Someone buying in a currency that weakens against USD gets fewer USDT for the same amount of local money than they would have a month earlier.

On the custody side, OKX states that user assets are held 1:1 and verified through regular Proof of Reserves reports. That's a disclosure you can check rather than take on faith; the reports are published.
